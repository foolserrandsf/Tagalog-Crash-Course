Learn first

Sentence frames

Tagalog runs on a handful of reusable shells: Gusto ko ng ___ (I want ___), May ___ ba? (Is there ___?), Nag-___ ako (I ___-ed). Real Filipinos speak Taglish, which drops English words straight into Tagalog frames. Own about 10 frames and English fills the blanks.

Ignore completely
  • Verb "focus" theory (actor vs. object focus, the full affix system). You'll use one verb shortcut instead.
  • Tagalog numbers above 10. Prices, time and addresses are usually said in English or Spanish-derived numbers.
  • "Pure" vocabulary. Nobody says salumpuwit for chair. They say upuan, or just "chair."
The one exercise

Write 15 sentences you actually say on a normal day. Rebuild each in a Tagalog frame, keeping the English nouns and verbs. Say all 15 aloud to a Filipino speaker, or record them and compare against a Taglish vlog. It's the last step in this app, and it saves as you type.

Step 1

Flip the sentence

English puts the person first: "I am tired." Tagalog puts the description or action first, then the person: "Tired I."

TagalogLiterallyMeans
Pagod ako.Tired II'm tired.
Gutom ka?Hungry you?Are you hungry?
Busy siya.Busy he/sheHe/she is busy.
Okay lang ako.Okay just II'm just fine.

Pronouns

akoIkamiwe (not you)
kayoutayowe (including you)
siyahe / shesilathey
kayoyou (plural, or polite to one older person)

Two small words

po shows respect to elders, strangers and customers. It goes right after the first word: Pagod po ako.

ba marks a yes/no question, same spot: Gutom ka ba?

Words: masaya happy · gutom hungry · pagod tired · handa ready · plus any English adjective.

Step 2

The verb shortcut: mag- + any English verb

Skip the affix system. Stick mag- on an English verb and change the front to show time. Person still goes after.

TimePatternExampleMeans
Base / "to"mag-Gusto kong mag-drive.I want to drive.
Done (past)nag-Nag-text ako.I texted.
Doing (now / habit)nag- + 1st syllable doubledNagwo-work ako.I'm working.
Will do (future)mag- + 1st syllable doubledMagco-cook ako mamaya.I'll cook later.

The doubling trick

Take the first consonant + vowel sound of the English verb and repeat it: work → wo-work · drive → da-drive · text → te-text · cook → co-cook. Spelling is loose; people write it how it sounds.

Time words: kanina earlier today · kahapon yesterday · mamaya later today · bukas tomorrow · ngayon now.

Step 3

Ten frames that cover most of a day

Learn these as chunks. Don't analyze them; swap the blank.

Survival lines

Salamat po. Thank you · Hindi ko alam. I don't know · Hindi ko gets. I don't get it · Dahan-dahan lang po. Slowly please · Oo / Opo yes / yes (polite) · Hindi po no (polite)

CR ("comfort room") is the everyday word for restroom.

Step 4

Saying no: hindi, wala, ayaw

Three "no" words, each for a different job. The one rule: the pronoun jumps forward, right after the no-word.

WordUse forPositiveNegative
hindinot (describing / doing)Pagod ako.Hindi ako pagod.
waladon't have / there isn'tMay time ako.Wala akong time.
ayawdon't wantGusto ko.Ayoko. / Ayaw ko.

Notice Wala akong time: when wala links to a noun, ako gets a -ng glued on. Same with Gusto kong…. If a word ends in a vowel and links to the next one, add -ng. Otherwise use na. That's all the grammar you need for it.

Step 5

Question words

Question word goes first. Then the same flipped order you already know.

Ano?What?Ano ang ginagawa mo?What are you doing?
Saan?Where?Saan ka pupunta?Where are you going?
Kailan?When?Kailan ka babalik?When are you coming back?
Sino?Who?Sino siya?Who is he/she?
Bakit?Why?Bakit ka pagod?Why are you tired?
Paano?How?Paano mag-order?How do you order?
Ilan?How many?Ilan kayo?How many of you?

Taglish shortcut: with an English verb, the mag- shortcut still works after a question word: Kailan ka magta-travel? When will you travel?
